# Comparing grammars

This chapter asks the question the earlier chapters prepare for: does the
grammar that generated a corpus fit it better than a rival grammar does? It
reads an [experiment document](../how-to/experiments/sweeps.md#declare-the-points) that crosses one source grammar with two
candidate learner grammars and two validity controls, prices the experiment
from timed training steps, trains its ten points as a pilot, and reduces their
rows to a verdict. The small corpora make this a local exercise; elapsed time
depends on the machine.

## The experiment document

Chapters 1 to 5 each trained one point from a [run document](../how-to/setup/configuration.md#the-words-this-site-uses). An experiment
document names a run document and declares a sweep over it, the root seed its
points derive their seeds from, the rows it reports and the reducers that turn
those rows into a result. The one this chapter uses is the reduced freeze of
the S2 experiment,
[`experiments/s2/reduced-freeze.yaml`](https://github.com/logical-form-induction/lofi/blob/main/experiments/s2/reduced-freeze.yaml),
over the run document [`experiments/s2/s2-run.yaml`](https://github.com/logical-form-induction/lofi/blob/main/experiments/s2/s2-run.yaml).

An experiment organizes several points around one question. A [sweep](../how-to/experiments/choose-a-sweep-design.md)
lists which settings vary across those points. Here we compare a source
grammar with a rival on data generated by the source, then run controls that
check whether apparent success survives changes meant to disrupt the relevant
structure. A grammar that fits sentences well need not be the grammar that
generated them: additional flexibility or a favorable encoding can also
improve fit. The comparison must state which alternatives it rules out.

The run document trains a tagged [chart parser](../how-to/models/configure-a-chart-parser.md#choose-the-chart-family) on a corpus drawn from the
source grammar `slashes-application-k2-depth1`: two primitives, the two slash
constructors, forward and backward application, and a generated [lexicon](../how-to/grammars/declare-a-lexicon.md#list-entries-inline) of
eight forms. Chapters 2 to 5 used the data's grammar for the learner. For
this comparison, the learner's grammar is declared under [`model.language`](../reference/spec.md#lofi.spec.ModelLanguageSpec), so the corpus and
the grammar the learner parses it with can vary independently:

```yaml
defaults:
  - data.datasets.train.dataset: s2-source-run
  - model.language: slashes-application-k2-depth1
  - model.spaces.categories.combinators: application
```

Its one evaluator, [`held-out`](../reference/spec.md#lofi.spec.HeldOutLikelihoodSpec), scores each point's held-out string
log likelihood per sentence and its description length.

Held-out sentences were not used to update the learner. Their [log
likelihood](../how-to/evaluation/evaluate-a-run.md#metric-denominators) is the logarithm of the probability the model assigns to them;
this metric averages it per sentence. Larger values, including less negative
values, mean better predictive fit. The [description length](../explanation/synthetic-designs.md#does-the-source-grammar-win) accounts for
the cost assigned to representing the candidate. The reducer uses both in the recovery criterion described below.

Before expanding the settings, the design has four roles:

| Data condition | Learner grammar | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original source | Source | How well does the matching grammar fit? |
| Original source | Rival | Does an alternative fit as well or better? |
| Reversed ground truth | Source | Does the declared reversal control behave as expected? |
| Scrambled lexicon | Source | Does disrupting lexical assignments change the comparison? |

The source grammar distinguishes arguments on the left from arguments on
the right. The rival uses an undirected arrow and [composition](../how-to/grammars/choose-combinatory-rules.md#the-rule-families), with a larger
primitive inventory. Those are explicit differences in their assumptions;
the long configuration names identify the exact declarations.

The experiment varies three groups together in a [`yoked` sweep](../how-to/experiments/sweeps.md#declare-the-points), so the
values at one position form one point:

```yaml
sweep:
  yoked:
    - - group: data.datasets.train.dataset
        values: [s2-source-run, s2-source-run, slashes-application-k2-depth1-reversed, slashes-application-k2-depth1-scrambled]
      - group: model.language
        values: [slashes-application-k2-depth1, arrow-composition-k4-depth2, slashes-application-k2-depth1, slashes-application-k2-depth1]
      - group: model.spaces.categories.combinators
        values: [application, arrow-composition, application, application]
      # ... and the comparison.source, comparison.condition and comparison.direction paths
  grid:
    - path: data.datasets.train.dataset.size
      values: [8]
  seeds: [0]
  expected_points: 10
signature_twins: {count: 3, seed: 109, primary_condition: primary, observed_twin: observed}
```

Reading the design:

| Part | What it says |
|---|---|
| First two positions | The source corpus under the source grammar itself, and under `arrow-composition-k4-depth2`, a grammar with one undirected arrow constructor, four primitives and composition. These are the `primary` cells. |
| Last two positions | Validity controls: the source grammar fitted to a corpus with its ground truth reversed, and to one with its lexicon scrambled. A sound comparison should not find the source grammar winning on either. |
| [`comparison`](../reference/spec.md#lofi.spec.SourceComparisonPointSpec) | Each point's role in the comparison: the source grammar's name, the condition and the direction of the candidate. The reducer reads these paths. |
| `signature_twins` | Each primary candidate is also fitted under three signature twins, drawn with the declared seed, and the reducer checks each grammar's observed fit against its twins' fits. |
| `size: 8` | Eight sentences per corpus, which keeps each point to a few seconds. |

Two primary cells with three twins each, plus their observed fits and the two
controls, give the ten declared points.

**Yoked** means that corresponding entries move together: the first dataset,
first learner grammar, and first combinator choice form one configuration,
then the second entries form another. A [**grid**](../how-to/experiments/choose-a-sweep-design.md#compare-the-designs) instead crosses the listed
choices independently. Here the grid supplies only one corpus size, so it
adds no further combinations.

A [signature twin](../explanation/synthetic-designs.md#does-the-source-grammar-win) reassigns the learner's primitive, constructor, and
combinator slots while retaining its inventories. Primitive and constructor
permutations are restricted to compatible shapes. Each assignment is trained
as its own point, with source data seeds held fixed during twin expansion.
Comparing these fits on the fixed corpus tests whether the observed assignment
has an advantage over the declared rearrangements. Model capacity remains a
separate requirement of the comparison. The
[source comparison explanation](../explanation/synthetic-designs.md) states
the criterion and its scope.

## Expanding the points

[`read_experiment`](../reference/run.md#lofi.run.read_experiment) reads and validates the document, and
[`expand_experiment`](../reference/run.md#lofi.run.expand_experiment) resolves one configuration per point, with each point's
identifier derived from its resolved configuration and seed:

```python
from pathlib import Path

from lofi.run import expand_experiment, read_experiment

document = Path("experiments/s2/reduced-freeze.yaml")
experiment = read_experiment(document)
points = expand_experiment(experiment, Path.cwd())

for point in points:
    comparison = point.config["comparison"]
    candidate = point.config["model"]["language"]["grammar"]["name"]
    print(point.point_id, candidate, comparison["condition"], comparison["twin"])
```

## Pricing the experiment

[`lofi price`](../how-to/experiments/sweeps.md#price-the-experiment) builds every point's training stack, times a few of its steps on
the named device, and writes a price record under the root it is given. Here
**price** estimates the compute time needed for the experiment.
Warmup runs allow setup work to settle before the timed repeats:

```bash
uv run lofi price \
  --experiment experiments/s2/reduced-freeze.yaml \
  --device cpu \
  --warmup 1 \
  --repeats 2 \
  --root .
```

Each line is one distinct training stack, with the mean wall time of its timed
steps. The p50 and p90 hours bootstrap the experiment's total from those
steps. `declared: unpriced` reports the document's own `compute` field; the
measured price is stored under the sweep's identifier. The timings vary from machine
to machine, and on a loaded machine from run to run.

The p50 estimate is the median of the resampled total times; p90 is their
90th percentile. These estimates depend on the timing model and its sample. With two repeats,
they illustrate the calculation; future execution times can fall outside them.

## Running the points

On a cluster, [`lofi launch`](../how-to/experiments/sweeps.md#launch-from-the-freeze) launches an experiment's points against a freeze:
a tagged commit that every point records as its source revision. Before any
freeze exists, the same command runs the experiment as a
pilot. `--pilot` takes the place of `--freeze`, and the points train one after
another in this process on the serial launcher:

```bash
uv run lofi launch \
  --experiment experiments/s2/reduced-freeze.yaml \
  --pilot \
  --out pilot/launch.json
```

The command echoes the launch record it writes to `pilot/launch.json`: ten
points offered, ten launched and none served from the store. Each point's
seed is derived from the experiment's root seed, 109, and its identifier, and
each point writes the same records as in chapter 3, under
`pilot/runs/<point-id>/`. Launching the pilot again serves every point from
the pilot store instead of training it again.

A pilot reads no freeze record and requires no tag, so every row it reports
is marked `unfrozen` and its results directory,
`pilot/results/reduced-freeze/`, lies apart from every frozen one. Ingestion
and a freeze refuse these results with [`PilotResultsError`](../reference/run.md#lofi.run.results.PilotResultsError); they
are for looking at a design before it is frozen.

A Git **commit** records a particular version of the source, and a **tag**
gives that version a name. Freezing an experiment makes its source revision
explicit. A [pilot](../how-to/experiments/sweeps.md#run-a-pilot-before-the-freeze) is a rehearsal for checking the design. Its rows are marked `unfrozen`. This
pilot runs locally before a release tag exists.

## Reading the rows

Every point reports the rows the experiment declares, and the document
declares one table over the primary row, the held-out string log likelihood
per sentence:

```yaml
reports:
  - name: held-out-fit
    rows: [held-out/string-log-likelihood-per-sentence]
    columns: [freeze_id, config_hash, split, n, value]
    labels: [Freeze, Configuration, Split, Sentences, Log-likelihood per sentence]
    precision: 3
```

[`lofi results --pilot`](../reference/cli.md#results) renders the declared tables from the pilot's rows and
prints where each one was written:

```bash
uv run lofi results --experiment experiments/s2/reduced-freeze.yaml --pilot
```

Each value counts two held-out sentences. At this size, the pilot checks the
measurement procedure; the source-recovery claim requires the tests below.

## Reducing to a verdict

The [`source-comparison` reducer](../reference/spec.md#lofi.spec.SourceComparisonReducerSpec) the document declares reads these rows
together with each point's manifest and measured capacity, all of which the
pilot's results directory holds, and tests the source grammar against each
candidate, each twin and each control:

```python
from pathlib import Path

import pyarrow.parquet as pq

from lofi.eval import ReductionContext, reduce_experiment_rows
from lofi.run import comparison_points, expand_experiment, read_experiment
from lofi.run.results import pilot_directory

document = Path("experiments/s2/reduced-freeze.yaml")
experiment = read_experiment(document)
points = expand_experiment(experiment, Path.cwd())
results = pilot_directory(Path.cwd(), experiment)

rows = pq.read_table(results / "rows.parquet").to_pylist()
measured = pq.read_table(results / "measured.parquet").to_pylist()
manifests = [
    experiment.config.manifest_point(
        (results / "manifests" / f"{point.point_id}.json").read_bytes()
    )
    for point in points
]

reduction = reduce_experiment_rows(
    rows,
    experiment.reducers,
    ReductionContext(
        document.parent,
        seed=experiment.seed_policy.root,
        points=comparison_points(manifests, measured),
        preregistration=experiment.preregistration,
        alpha=experiment.evaluation.alpha,
        correction=experiment.evaluation.correction,
        dependence=experiment.evaluation.dependence,
    ),
)
for row in reduction.rows:
    value = row.get("value")
    shown = value if not isinstance(value, float) else round(value, 3)
    print(row["reducer"], row.get("candidate", row.get("winner")), shown)
```

```text
source-comparison-delta arrow-composition-k4-depth2 -0.432
source-comparison-test arrow-composition-k4-depth2 -0.432
source-comparison-test reversed-ground-truth 0.102
source-comparison-test lexicon-scrambled -0.114
source-comparison-twin arrow-composition-k4-depth2 0.667
source-comparison-twin slashes-application-k2-depth1 0.333
source-comparison-verdict arrow-composition-k4-depth2 None
source-by-winner arrow-composition-k4-depth2 1
source-win-threshold None None
```

The reducer combines three kinds of input: metric rows, manifests identifying
the trained configurations, and measured capacities. The `.parquet` files
store tables; `pyarrow` reads them into rows for this Python example. This
keeps the statistical comparison tied to the runs that supplied its evidence.

The **winner** is the candidate selected by the measured comparison score.
A **positive** verdict requires the source to satisfy all the declared tests,
including controls. The verdict row records which conditions the source
grammar met:

```python
[verdict] = (
    row for row in reduction.rows if row["reducer"] == "source-comparison-verdict"
)
for key in (
    "winner",
    "source_rank",
    "beats_candidates",
    "beats_twins",
    "validity_controls_pass",
    "positive",
):
    print(key, verdict[key])
```

```text
winner arrow-composition-k4-depth2
source_rank 2
beats_candidates False
beats_twins False
validity_controls_pass False
positive False
```

Reading the reduction:

| Row | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `source-comparison-delta` | The source score minus the candidate score, after the declared description length charge per held-out sentence: negative, so the candidate scores better at this size. |
| `source-comparison-test` | One test per rival, the candidate and each control, corrected across the family at the document's `alpha`. With one seed and two held-out sentences, none rejects. |
| `source-comparison-twin` | The twin check for each grammar. The source grammar's observed fit has value 0.333, and the candidate's has value 0.667; neither passes. |
| `source-comparison-verdict` | The source grammar ranks second, beats neither its twins nor the candidate, and the controls do not pass, so the verdict is not positive. |
| `source-by-winner` | One count in the cell for this source and this winner, the row a full experiment sums into a source-by-winner matrix. |

A positive verdict needs every condition at once: the source grammar beats each
candidate, beats its twins, and the controls behave. At eight sentences and one
seed the reduced freeze checks only that the pipeline produces each row; the
S2 production document, [`experiments/s2/source-complexity.yaml`](https://github.com/logical-form-induction/lofi/blob/main/experiments/s2/source-complexity.yaml), crosses eight
source grammars with eight candidates at three corpus sizes and five seeds.

The document's [`alpha`](../explanation/experiments-and-commitments.md#paired-tests-and-equivalence) sets the statistical threshold used by its tests.
Because several contrasts are tested together, the [declared correction](../explanation/experiments-and-commitments.md#corrections)
adjusts for that family of comparisons. Neither a favorable mean difference
nor being ranked first substitutes for meeting these conditions. The small
pilot is useful precisely because it can finish successfully while returning
`positive False`.

Once the experiment is frozen, [`lofi launch --freeze`](../reference/cli.md#launch) runs the
same points against the tag, [`lofi ingest`](../reference/cli.md#ingest) accepts their results against the
freeze and applies the same reducer, and [`lofi results`](../reference/cli.md#results) without `--pilot`
renders the same tables from the frozen results directory.
